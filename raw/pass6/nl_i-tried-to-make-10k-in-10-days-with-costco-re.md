Let me walk you through exactly how this works so you can copy it this week:

**What You're Doing:**

Big retailers get a flood of returns and overstock every single day. Costco can't put a customer-returned washer back on the floor, and they don't want it sitting in a warehouse either. So **_they sell it off in bulk at a massive discount to people like us_**. You buy the pallet, you sell the items individually on Facebook Marketplace at roughly half of retail, and you keep the difference. If it sells in a store, it can be bought as a return.

**Get On The Auction Site and Learn The Grades**

Most of this product moves through liquidation auction platforms like B-Stock, which is where Shannon and I did our buying. You log in and see hundreds of live auctions across every category you can imagine, from appliances to coffee to musical instruments.

Before you bid a dollar, understand the condition grades, because this is where beginners get burned.   
-"New" and "Like New" are what they sound like.   
-**"Used Good" means customer returns, and that's the sweet spot. Someone bought it, changed their mind, sent it back, and it's basically fine.**  
-"Used Fair" starts getting into real issues, so steer clear early on.   
-And avoid anything labeled "Mixed" or "Salvage." That can be almost anything, and a lot of it is junk.

**Pick A Category and Do The Math First**

Shannon likes appliances because they're big, they're heavy, and most people won't mess with them. Those barriers scare off competition, which means less bidding and better prices for you. Outdoor gear like patio sets, grills, and sheds is another strong lane, because demand spikes the moment the weather turns.

The research is what keeps you safe. **Before you place a bid, open the manifest and see what's on the pallet.**  
1. Look for items still in the box with factory stickers, since those are the easiest to sell. 2. Check Facebook Marketplace and Lowe's to see what these items really move for used in your area.   
3. Bid low enough that one or two sales cover your entire investment.

We paid $243 a unit on appliances worth four to five times that. Our safety net was _enormous._

**Store It**

You don’t need a warehouse. Shannon has 2,200 people in his community and most of them run this out of their garage. And a 10x10 storage unit runs a bit over $100 a month and the first month is often free. Plenty of facilities will even unload the truck for you.

Same goes for delivery. Rent a truck from Home Depot for around $20, or find a local guy who'll run deliveries for $50 a pop. _Offering free local delivery also becomes a selling point later._

**List Fast and Right**

This is where the money is made or lost. When your pallet arrives, resist the urge to fix the broken stuff first. Get your best-looking items listed immediately.

Take a quick video walking around each item, then upload photos. Use every photo slot Facebook gives you, and show the flaws too. A scratch you disclose builds trust. A scratch you hide gets you a bad review.

For the listing itself, let ChatGPT do the heavy lifting. **_Ask it to write an SEO Facebook Marketplace title and description with the model number, color, and key features._** Then add two or three sentences in your own voice about why you're doing this, whether that's saving for your kids or building something on the side. _That personal note creates a connection a generic ad never will._ Drop the retail price and a link to the Costco or Lowe's listing right in the description so buyers can see the deal for themselves.

**Sell With Speed**

Momentum matters more than perfection here. The stuff that sold fastest for me was stuff I never expected (like garment steamers I didn't even know existed). So get things listed, and let the market tell you what's hot.

When inquiries come in, and most arrive as "Is this still available?" over Messenger, respond fast and respond with a next step. Don't just say yes. Say, "_Yes, I can meet you at 3pm today, does that work?_" Build a few templates and keyboard shortcuts so you're not retyping the same answers all day.

When something isn't getting attention, boost the listing, which gives mixed results, or cut the price. Be willing to cut. Storing this stuff costs money, and _a smaller profit always beats a gazebo camping out in your garage._

**What This Costs/ Returns**

My challenge was $10,000 in profit in 10 days. It took me about twelve and a half, _and I only missed the timeline because I procrastinated on listing and responding._

To get started, you need a credit card, a garage or a cheap storage unit, and an internet connection. Your first pallet can pay you back with one or two sales, and everything after that is margin.
