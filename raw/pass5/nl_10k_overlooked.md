### Step 1: What You’re Selling

The product is an aluminum sandwich board with a dry erase coating, rated for indoor/ outdoor use, sitting on a stand. The restaurant's logo goes at the top. The middle is open space where the owner writes their specials, lunch and dinner deals, or upcoming events. **_The bottom has six small printed ads for other local businesses._**

The restaurant gets a better looking specials board than the tiny chalkboard they were using, **_completely free_**, and you get six ad slots to sell. Two different sets of customers, one piece of aluminum- very old school and simple.

### Step 2: Get One Board Printed

Find a local print shop and get a quote on a single dry erase aluminum board. Rachel pays about $350 each near her, and there is no minimum order, so you can print one at a time and reprint as you sell more. Their turnaround runs about two weeks.

Add the stand and the markers and your all-in cost lands around $400. What keeps your risk near zero: **_you do not print anything until you have sold the ad spots._**You’re not fronting money on inventory. You only pay for the board once the money is already collected.

### Step 3: Get Host Location Before Selling Anything

Do not sell any ads before you have a home for the board. Advertisers want to know where their ad is going, so the host has to come first.

**_You’re looking for high foot traffic where people sit and wait._**Restaurants are the sweet spot, and coffee shops and gyms work too. Skip the low traffic waiting rooms like a dentist or physical therapy office, where twenty people might see it all day.

Message ten to fifteen local restaurants on Instagram. Tell them you are looking for a local spot to host one of these boards for free, they came highly recommended, and would they take a look. Attach a photo so they instantly get what you are offering. Roughly one in five replies. When they ask you to stop by and you show up with the actual board, the close is nearly automatic. It is free for them, it looks good, and it solves a problem they already have. (Somewhere to write their daily specials!)

### Step 4: Ask For Referrals On The Spot

Get a simple agreement in place. The restaurant keeps the board up in a visible location for one year, and if they sell the business, the agreement transfers to the new owner. That protects your advertisers.

Then, before you leave, ask the owner who should be on the board. Get specific. “Who handles your insurance?” “Who is your attorney?” “Last time your HVAC went out, who did you call?” “Is there a realtor who eats lunch here every week you would want to give a shot?” Those pointed prompts pull names out of someone who can’t think of anyone

In a perfect world. You should walk out with twelve to twenty referrals.

### Step 5: Fill Six Spots

Call the referrals. Rachel closes about six out of every twelve - twenty, (which is exactly what she needs to fill a board.) **_Sell each of the six spots for $600 a year, collected upfront. That is $3,600 per board against about $400 in cost._**

She says the easiest businesses to close are tax offices, insurance agents, and realtors. They already understand branding advertising and do not obsess over tracing a single sale back to it. _When someone asks about ROI, explain it like a billboard. If the restaurant sees 7,000 to 10,000 customers a month, that is how many people your ad sits in front of_. At $600 a year, that works out to about a $30 cost per thousand views, which is a genuinely good rate next to a billboard or Facebook ads.

Pro-tip: Offer a business two locations for $900 total instead of $600 for one. Double the exposure, and you have a natural reason for them to spend more.

### Step 6: Print, Place, Collect

Now that the spots are sold, send your designs to the printer. You can make the ads yourself in Canva or ChatGPT and include the design in the price, or let advertisers submit their own. Once the board is printed, place it at the host location and send each advertiser a photo of their ad live in the wild. Then repeat the whole cycle with your next host.

### Step 7 (Bonus!): Turn Boards Into Recurring Revenue

This is an extension to the original sign hustle. The reason Rachel's business does $10,000 to $15,000 a month is what happens **_after_**the board goes up.

Once a business has paid you and watched you deliver, you have trust. _When you send an advertiser the photo of their live board, mention that you noticed a few gaps on their website or Google profile you could help with. Now you are pitching websites, which run $1,000 to $6,000 to build, and reputation management at around $400 a month._
