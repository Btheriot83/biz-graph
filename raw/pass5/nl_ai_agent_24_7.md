Here is how I turned that into something I can sell, and _a few different ways you can do the same._

1.   **Fix your own annoyance/ issue first.** The idea came straight out of a problem I deal with every single day. I cannot stay out of my inbox. So I had it build an app that locks Gmail and my Mail app outside of one set window every morning. If I do not get to inbox zero by 10, I do not get another shot until tomorrow. I built it for me. But I know I am not the only person who fights this, and that is why I know it can be successful. When something annoys you enough that you would pay to make it stop, thousands of other people feel the exact same way. _Those people are your customers._

2.   **Write the prompt with a second AI before you hand it over.** I did not dump my rough idea straight into Grok bot. I wrote it up in Claude first and had Claude clean it into a real, technical prompt. **Do this.** No matter what you idea is.A good planning AI catches what the builder will not. Claude told me flat out that a website can never force-quit an app on my Mac, so the real product had to be a Mac menu bar app plus a Chrome extension. That pushback saved me from building something that was never going to work in the first place.

1.   **Treat it like an employee.** A good boss hands somebody one clear job and walks away. A bad boss hovers and says do this, now try that, now this other thing. I gave Grok bot a single focus and left it alone. I didn’t connect it to a bunch of accounts it did not need. I set a spending cap so it could approve its own Replit charges up to a hundred dollars a day without stopping to ask me every five minutes. It is really important to set up boundaries, but then also give it room to work.

2.   **Point it at the projects you gave up on.** Somewhere in the middle of the build, another use for this jumped out at me. Most of us have three or four half-built things sitting at 90 percent that we abandoned- because the last 10 percent was a slog. **Send Grok bot into your Replit account (or whatever builder you use) and tell it to finish them.** The stuff you already quit on might be a weekend away from being sellable.

Small business owners pay _good_ money for simple tools that fix one specific headache they have, and now you can build that tool in an afternoon and charge them for it every month.
